Funding Facts

Documenting foreign funding flows into Israeli civil society and politics — with a focus on European political foundations.

Purpose: Make the scale, mechanisms, and recipients of government-linked European funding visible — especially to audiences in Europe.

The Core Issue

Critics frequently accuse Israel of foreign interference in Western politics. These accusations rarely come with comparable documentation. Meanwhile, European governments — particularly Germany — have for years channeled substantial public funds into Israeli civil society organizations, research centers, and advocacy groups that shape discourse inside Israel.

This project collects and presents publicly available information about those funding flows. The goal is transparency, not conspiracy theories.

Core Findings

Germany has been one of the largest foreign governmental sources of funding to left-leaning and political advocacy NGOs in Israel over the past decade.

Significant sums are channeled through political foundations (Stiftungen) affiliated with German parties represented in the Bundestag. These foundations receive the large majority of their budgets from the German federal government, allocated roughly according to the parliamentary strength of the affiliated parties.

Additional channels include church-related aid organizations and programs such as the Civil Peace Service (Forum ZFD).

Key reported figures

Primarily based on 2012–2022 data compiled by Im Tirtzu and covered by Israeli media investigations:

  • At least NIS 150 million from German sources to left-wing and anti-Zionist organizations over roughly a decade.
  • Of which approximately NIS 30 million came via the German political foundations.

These figures should always be verified against the original reports. Reporting practices vary and some foundations have been criticized for incomplete disclosure.

How the Money Flows

German Federal Budget
        ↓
Political Foundations (Stiftungen)  +  Forum ZFD  +  Church aid organizations
        ↓
Israeli NGOs, research centers, dialogue projects, legal advocacy, and public events
        ↓
Influence on public discourse, legal strategies, and (in some alleged cases) political campaigning

The foundations are formally independent. In practice they receive the bulk of their funding from the German state and operate with a clear political orientation linked to their parent parties.

The Legal Gray Area

Israeli law prohibits political parties from receiving foreign donations or in-kind contributions. Direct foreign funding of election campaigns is illegal.

Funding NGOs and “independent” centers that engage in political advocacy sits in a gray area. Critics argue that this structure allows foreign governments to influence Israeli politics indirectly while remaining formally compliant with the letter of the law. Supporters of the funding argue that supporting civil society is legitimate and distinct from party financing.

This distinction — and the repeated investigations into whether the line has been crossed — is central to the public debate.

Recent Case (November 2026)

A Channel 14 investigation raised allegations that suppliers for election rallies of “The Democrats” party were directed to receive payment via the Willy Brandt Center in Jerusalem. The center is funded by Forum ZFD, an organization that receives direct German government funding.

If confirmed, such arrangements would raise serious questions under Israel’s Party Financing Law regarding foreign in-kind contributions to a political campaign. The case is still developing and should be followed through primary reporting.

Why This Matters for Europeans

German and other European taxpayers fund these activities. Understanding where public money goes — and what political effects it may have inside another democratic country — is a legitimate matter of public interest and democratic accountability.

This site exists to make the available information clearer and more accessible.
